# RED TAG RUMBLE: handoff

A split-screen bot builder and arena combat cart for wasmcart. One to four pads, three.c for the 3D, a CPU painted HUD composited in a second pass, Bellows for the SFX, baked WAV stems for the music, a deterministic 60 Hz sim that also builds natively for the balance tool.

The complete specification is `docs/design/FINAL-bots.md`. Read section 20 (things an implementer must not get wrong) before touching anything. `docs/design/combat-brief.md` is the research it came from. The platform, audio and HUD maps live beside the spec in the planning folder of the parent checkout.

## Status

The DEEP overhaul (October 2026, on main) answered the owner's playtest: "it's not clear how to attack; battle sound effects aren't very good; I want damage to show; I want models to be better; I want weapons to damage how they would; I want wedge physics to work; sometimes there are robots on the field that don't register as rigid bodies". The plan is `docs/design/DEEP-plan.md` (with the four lens designs beside it); four branches were merged in its order and their worktrees removed:

- sim-physics: bodies with clearance, height, front and back lips, pitch and roll; drive as grip limited force with sideways slides; contacts with the full angular term (the old torque sign was backwards); the wedge as physics (underride, ride up, carry, slip, the deterministic tip, wedge clash, DIG IN on the weapon key); spinner energy and bite with the wedge glance; the hammer arc and the spike stroke; flips decided by where the victim sits on the plate and landings by pitch; saw grind, crusher hold, flame burn, lifter hold; wheel stations; per panel armour pools that dent, bend, hang and fall off; the HUD readiness fields; corpses that still take hits as props and bots that always collide on the ground (the two sim causes of ghost bots); `build/balance --scenario`.
- graphics: no ghost bots (the renderer no longer draws empty sim slots); vertex coloured bots with bevelled, bolted plates, every chassis, drive and weapon rebuilt with READY, TELL, STRIKE and RECOVER poses; damage that shows (dented, bent, hanging, gone, with the internals behind a gone plate, decals, smoke and fire by HP); weapon specific spark spray and the impact star; the KO column and debris; spinner blur discs and floor danger rings; pitch and roll on the bots; arena shadows, lexan reflections, tyre marks and hazard floor tells; a draw budget of under 60 calls and 15k triangles per view.
- controls-hud: every keycap names the key the pad will press (`src/keys.c`); a weapon widget that says when and how to attack; hit markers in the attacker's seat colour and rust for the victim, with the reason a hit was weak; the weak side chevron; a three beat attack coach on the first fight of a fresh save (save version 2); self right prompts per build; BOOST and BRAKE words.
- audio: noise first impacts with velocity from the sim, a voice for every weapon tell and strike, weapon, drive, damage and hazard loops driven once a frame from the match (`audio_fight_update`), a crowd, a duck sized by hit band, and a bake that measures every target and fails on a miss; the arena music rebaked.

The integration pass on top of the merge: the event drain in main.c with its merge aid ifdefs removed (every sim event reaches fx, HUD, audio, rumble and the camera), the patches the implementers listed (the save upgrade, seat 1 starting on LAUNCH, the d-pad substitution, shot.mjs pad names, the hudtest link, the how-to lint), and what the keyboard feel gate in Chrome turned up: the EASY practice bot hits a person for half (`PRACTICE_PULL`, sim.c), a spinner lands at most one blow per pair every 45 frames (`SPINNER_HIT_CD`, was 30: a held spinner on a slow bot was a metronome of two hits a second), the stinger bar sits low over the strip instead of across the bot at 35 percent of the view (it covered a flipped bot at the top of its arc), the impact star's canvas no longer draws a solid square, the six drive laps re-recorded for the grip limited drive, and `tools/play.mjs` became the feel gate driver.

The lifter and polish pass (October 2026, on main): the fix/lifter branch merged (its lip rename was already on main). The LIFTER is a real weapon: hold the weapon key to arm with the forks low, a bot touching the forks from any side is grabbed and raised, letting go at full lift tips it over (it lands by the flipper's landing rules), letting go early sets it down gently, a carried bot can be dropped into a pit or off an edge, and a wedge front deflects the forks; the LIFTER AI arms from the side or rear, tips tall bots and carries the rest to a drop; the widget reads HOLD, LIFT, LET GO: TIP and CARRY; nine lift rows in `--scenario` and `--robin-build N c,d,w,a` (one build against the six presets). Then the rough edges of risk 7: every status stinger (AIRBORNE, WEDGED, BIG HIT and the rest) is a small tag, 36 px tall in a full or half view and 30 in a quadrant, centred in a status slot just above the strip, under the bot; the slot holds one thing at a time by priority (the count, then a stinger, then the coach chip), stingers that arrive while it is busy queue (three deep, stale after 1.5 s, a repeat merges) and a view shows one reason word at a time; the count moved out of the viewport centre into that slot as a numeral and the self right prompt on one plate; the KO burst flies at full speed through the hit stop, the slow motion and the hold, 2.2 times wider than a fight shed (`sim_ko_scatter`, `sim_debris_step`, only after the match is over); a bot that lands inverted over its nose or tail turns its heading by half a turn with the residual pitch negated and its previous pose snapped, so the renderer shows no yaw pop (scenario flip-heading); EASY backs off a person at a wall for 5 s after a landed hit and waits 5 u off them toward the middle (scenario easy-wall-breath: 4 hits in 30 s, 7 with the old breath); the scene holds 258 objects in a 1v1 and 288 in a 4p fight (was 368): hidden bots leave the scene graph, the pickup rings are one instanced mesh, an arena's lit surround is one mesh per glow, and the vis cell groups are made only when a prop lands in them.

- `src/game_flow.c` runs the phase machine of 10.1: boot, title with the attract fight, settings, hall of fame, the rules flash, lobby, garage, arena vote, countdown, fight with pause, KO, judges, results and the rematch vote. Every phase change logs `[phase] N at frame F`.
- `src/game.c` keeps the state that has no renderer in it (the save struct with its crc, seats, the settings rows) so `tools/hudtest.c` still links it natively. Keep renderer, camera and audio calls out of it.
- Seats are packed when the lobby closes: seat i is match slot i and turntable i for the rest of the evening. In TOURNAMENT each pair plays in slots 0 and 1 with `bot_t.seat` set to the real seat.
- `main.c` drain_events turns every sim event into fx, audio, rumble, camera shake and punch, damage numbers and stingers. The station build logs one `[hits] f= n= dmg= rumble=` summary per sim second (right after the `[sim]` hash line, only when something happened); the per event `[hit]` and `[rumble]` lines are an `EXTRA_CFLAGS=-DRTR_TRACE` build, which also logs `[hud]` rect and `[memt]` heap lines once a second and `[mem]` at every phase change. Feel numbers live in juice.h; sim.h STOP_* mirror its J_STOP_* column and a static assert in main.c keeps them equal.
- The perf pass (the October audit): the HUD uploads only what changed (see Platform facts), the 30 fps fallback releases and resets per phase, every rebuild leak is closed (the soak below), the floor textures no longer copy at first upload, the material cache compares the whole tuple and grows, and the cart builds with `-Wshadow` (and clean under `-Wextra -Wno-unused-parameter`, which is not on by default).
- Hazard seats, the mini-KO hand-over to the hazard seat view, the TOURNAMENT bracket with the 3 player bye coin flip and the final's OPS crew live in game_flow.c.
- The pause menu belongs to the pad that paused; other pads cannot walk its cursor.

## How to run each tool

```sh
./build.sh                      # the cart: build/red-tag-rumble.wasc, copied to web/
./build.sh balance              # build/balance, native, headless sources only
./build.sh music                # rebake assets/music/*.wav; prints the deep-audio 6.1 and 6.2 table and exits 1 on any FAIL (0 FAILS today)
SFX_ONLY=1 build/bake_music <dir> <dir>   # the measurements without rebaking the stems
./build.sh lint                 # the house lint
REGEN_STATES=1 ./build.sh       # regenerate src/gen/rtr_gl.c from tools/states.txt

build/balance --parts                       # every preset's cost, bars and name
build/balance --robin 20 [driver=ai] [arenas=bay] [hazards=on|off|both] [verbose=1]
build/balance --check-arenas | --hashcheck | --modes 4
build/balance --scenario all | <name>       # the DEEP-plan 6.8 physics rows, PASS or FAIL with their numbers, non zero exit on a FAIL
build/balance --replay tools/scripts/drive-bay.json --setup "<the [sim] setup line from the cart log>"
build/balance --trace bay "ROLL OUT" SPIN 3000
build/balance --map bay out.ppm tools/scripts/drive-bay.json
build/balance --make-drive bay tools/scripts/drive-bay.json
build/balance --make-fight tools/scripts/2p-fight.json --setup "<the [sim] setup line>"   # record a real AI fight as a rig fixture
build/balance --replay tools/scripts/dpad-turn.json --setup "..." verbose=2              # verbose=2 prints the headings, verbose=3 every event ([ev] render= sim= TYPE a= b= zone= dmg= panel= flags= reason=)
node tools/serve.mjs 8797 & node tools/play.mjs 'http://127.0.0.1:8797/?v=1' build/play  # the feel gate: three keyboard fights in a real Chrome
node tools/wavstat.mjs build/out.wav 1      # per second peak, RMS, band shares and flatness of a WAV capture; FROM_FRAME=668 applies the three deep-audio 6.3 gates

cc -std=gnu11 -Wall -Wextra -I src tools/test_parts.c -o build/test_parts && build/test_parts
cc -std=gnu11 -O2 -Wall -DRTR_HEADLESS -I src src/sim.c src/bot.c src/arena.c src/ai.c tools/aitest.c -lm -o build/aitest && build/aitest
tools/hudtest.sh [scene]        # native HUD scenes to build/shots/menus-<scene>.png (HUD_DIV=2 for half size)

node tools/shot.mjs build/red-tag-rumble.wasc build/shots/x 120,260,900 tools/scripts/2p-fight.json
STATION=1 ...                   # only pad 0 reports connected, as the RetroArch core does
WAV=build/out.wav ...           # the audio the cart played
SAVE_OUT=build/s.sav ...        # the save region at the end of the run
SAVE_IN=build/s.sav ...         # boot with a save (never by default, so shots stay reproducible)
PACE=1 ...                      # real time, non-deterministic
```

`tools/play.mjs` launches its own Chrome with a throwaway profile (a fresh save), plays the menus by keyboard, then three fights against the EASY practice bot: LAUNCH (the coach, flips), CHOP (one S tap: spin up, side hits) and ROLL OUT (five more S taps: the underride). It steers by the cart's read only `rtr_probe` export (main.c: the phase and each slot's pose, HP, inverted, airborne, ride_by, spin, cooldown and worst panel), which a script injected before the page loads reaches by keeping the wasm instance. It writes PNGs at the menu steps, a screencast JPEG every 3rd rendered frame through each fight (`fight<n>/<seq>-f<sim frame>.jpg`), `probe.jsonl` (one line per driver tick, to find the frames of a flip or a hit) and `log.txt`. The driver is a stand in for a person: it closes and fires, holds Z and comes at a side, or comes at the front and digs in, backs off a stalled shove and keeps driving upside down. Kill the server afterwards.

Every balance knob behind an `#ifndef` takes a `-D` override, so a sweep builds several balance binaries without editing the tree (`cc -O2 -std=gnu11 -ffp-contract=off -DRTR_HEADLESS -DSPINNER_HIT_CD=60 -I src src/sim.c src/bot.c src/arena.c src/ai.c tools/balance.c -lm -o build/balance_x`): in sim.c RAM_BASE, FLIP_LAND_DMG, MELTY_HIT_CD, MELTY_TIP, CLASH_FRAC, CLASH_CD, TIP_HORIZ, TIP_DRUM, SPINNER_HIT_CD, BITE_BASE, BITE_SLOPE, BITE_MAX, KNOCK_HORIZ, SPIN_E_REF, HOVER_BRAKE, PRACTICE_PULL, TOOTH_MU; in sim.h SPINNER_HIT_MIN, RAM_MIN_CLOSING, RAM_CD; in parts.h the WP_MELTY_* four, LAT_GRIP_TWO, LAT_GRIP_FOUR, LAT_GRIP_TREAD, TUMBLE_LOWBOY, TUMBLE_BOX, V_TIP_BOX, V_TIP_LUNCHBOX.

## Physics model

The sim (src/sim.c, headless, deterministic: float, `-ffp-contract=off`, the trig table, fixed pair order, no allocation, no dice in any contact path) after the DEEP overhaul. The deep-physics lens design in docs/design is the long form.

- Bodies: each bot is a box (or a SHELL circle) with a ground clearance per drive, a height per chassis, front and back lip heights (WEDGE chassis 0.02, WEDGE plate 0.04, lowered LIFTER forks 0.05, LOWBOY nose 0.08; an inverted bot has none), pitch and roll with their rates, and a tip angle per chassis. Pitch and roll integrate every step and are interpolated for the renderer like the heading.
- Drive: a grip limited wheel force (`mu m g load`), lateral grip per drive (TREADS 1.6, SIX 1.2, FOUR 1.0, TWO 0.8, HOVER 0.08), so a hard sideways knock slides a TWO WHEEL bot and barely moves TREADS; EV_SLIDE over 2 u/s. `load` falls when a bot is ridden, so a bot on a wedge loses its push.
- Contacts: two grounded bots always collide (unless exactly one is on the deck); otherwise their height intervals must overlap. The full angular term with the right torque sign, restitution 0.2 between bots and 0.3 off walls, wall scrapes. A counted out or flipped out corpse stays a prop that weapons still hit for no damage (EVF_CORPSE).
- The wedge: a contact is PLAIN, UNDERRIDE, WEDGE_CLASH or RAM. A lip within 35 degrees of head on, closing over 0.5 u/s, under the victim's clearance slides under it: `ride_up` climbs the slope, the victim's nose rises, it loses load, a hit on it is EXPOSED (1.5x, 2.0x for the rider's 2ND weapon). Fast enough against a tall chassis (`V_TIP_*`: LUNCHBOX 2.2, FRAME 3.0, BOX 3.4 u/s) it tips over the top; slower it is carried for 90 frames and slides off. The sides of a wheeled or tracked bot are blocked. DIG IN (the weapon key on a WEDGE or no weapon build): 20 frames at 1.6x accel with the lip pressed down, 90 frame cooldown. A ram is 9 to 18 on entry, once per RAM_CD.
- Spinners: the disc stores `spin^2`; a hit takes a bite of 35 to 80 percent of it (more against a bot driving in) and the damage is that energy; a wedge front or a SHELL takes a quarter bite and the spinner glances off (EVF_DEFLECT, a hop and a knock back). Nothing bites under SPINNER_HIT_MIN (0.5 spin); one blow per pair per SPINNER_HIT_CD (45 frames). Walls and clashes cost spin. EV_SPIN_CHANGE reports every loss and SPIN_ARMED.
- Swings: the hammer head follows a 110 to minus 10 degree arc and does `20 x p` (p the share of the swing done), into the TOP plate at 1.25x, so it meets a tall bot early and soft and a low bot late and hard; the handle inside 0.35 u does 0.3x; a miss costs 4. The spike is full damage on the way out (frames 1 to 5) and 0.3x on the way back.
- Flips: the launch speed and the tumble come from where the victim sits on the plate and its chassis; the landing is decided by pitch (under 60 degrees on its wheels, over 120 inverted, between a bounce that decides on the next landing); landing on a bot does 8 to it. A LOWBOY standing still lands upright. A face change over the nose or tail (not a roll) turns the heading by half a turn and negates the residual pitch, because the bot now faces back the way it came.
- Panels: every bot has five plate pools by armour (PLY 20, ALU 28, UHMW 24, STEEL 36, TI 32, WEAR 44). The damage after armour wears the plate it landed on; at 70, 45, 20 and 0 percent the plate is dented, bent, hanging and gone (EV_PANEL_WEAR, then EV_SHED with the debris). A gone plate has no armour and takes 1.25x (EVF_STRIPPED). FOUR WHEEL loses a wheel at 30 HP, TREADS throw a track. No weapon hit takes more than HIT_CAP (a fifth) of a bot.
- The practice bot: an EASY AI in a match with a person hits that person for PRACTICE_PULL (0.5) of the capped damage, and after a hit on a person at a wall backs off for AI_EASY_WALL_BREATH (300 frames, ai.c) to a point 5 u off them toward the middle; AI against AI is untouched, so the robin does not see it.

## Event model

`event_t` (src/sim.h, DEEP-plan 2) carries `type, a, b, zone, x z y, dmg, nx nz` plus `panel` (the PN_* plate), `weapon` (WP_* or the EVW_* sentinels for melty, hazard, OPS, landing and wall), `flags` (EVF_CORPSE, DEFLECT, WHEEL, STRIPPED, TOP), `reason` (the one HIT_WHY_* word the HUD shows), `speed` (closing speed) and `j` (impulse). The DEEP events after EV_RESPAWN: EV_UNDERRIDE, EV_RIDE_END, EV_WEDGE_CLASH, EV_WHEEL_HIT, EV_PANEL_WEAR, EV_SCRAPE, EV_GRIND, EV_BURN_START, EV_BURN_END, EV_SPIN_CHANGE, EV_SLIDE, EV_LIFT, EV_LIFT_DROP, EV_FALL; EV_WEAPON_TELL zones are the TELL_* enum. The per event payload table is DEEP-plan 2.5. `sim_hash` is unchanged (x, z, h, y, hp and the live debris).

`main.c drain_events` is the one place every event fans out, after each sim step:

- a banded event (a hit, a ram, a clash, a KO): `fx_hit` (spray by weapon, panel and flags, the impact star), the camera shake and punch, `audio_hit` by kind, band, velocity, armour and attacker with `audio_duck_band` and a crowd reaction, rumble, the damage number in every view (the attacker's seat colour, rust for the victim, bone elsewhere), `hit_marker_all`, `strip_taken`, `strip_dealt`, the reason word (`hit_mark_add`), `world_bot_hit` for the dent and decal on a heavy hit, the floor scorch on a KO, the BIG HIT stinger, then `game_on_event` for the tallies.
- everything else: `fx_event`, then a case per type (launch whoosh and trail, landing thud, wall clank, underride and the WEDGED stinger, the ride drop, wedge clash, wheel hit and LIMPING, dent, shed and the strip's gone side, burst, scrape, grind, burn, spin change, slide, lift, fall, tells with the DIG IN camera dip, boost, self right, stuck, benches, bumpers, inversions, count ticks, hazard tells and hits, OPS, red tag, pickups, taunts, the hill, respawns, mini-KOs, the closing music).

The loops are not events: `audio_fight_update(&M)` runs once per render frame in PH_FIGHT and PH_KO (not while paused) and reads spin, saw, fuel, clamp, lift, riding, dig, grind, burn, HP, speed, airborne, inverted and the hazards; `audio_fight_update(NULL)` once on leaving silences them. The renderer reads pitch, roll, ride_up, panel states and the readiness fields straight from `bot_t` in `world_sync`.

## The header contract

The headers in `src/` are the contract between the subsystem agents, who implement in parallel without talking to each other. Rules:

1. A header is owned by one agent (the table below). Another agent who needs a new entry point adds the declaration to that header with a one line comment and leaves the body to the owner, or adds it to their own file and declares it in their own header. Never change a signature that is already declared; add a new one.
2. Every shared type is complete in the header it lives in. `sim.h` carries the 4.3 data verbatim; `parts.h` carries the whole catalogue and every build rule as `static inline`; `content.h` carries every string; `juice.h` carries every feel constant.
3. The headless line: `sim.c`, `bot.c`, `arena.c`, `ai.c`, `parts.h`, `content.h`, `juice.h` and `audio.h` include nothing from three.c, hud.h or wasmcart and use no libm trig. `./build.sh balance` enforces both with a dependency scan and a grep, and it is what the native tool and the replay depend on.
4. Strings live in `content.h` or `parts.h`, feel numbers in `juice.h`, nothing inline. `./build.sh lint` checks the house rules (ASCII caps, no dashes, no emoji, glyph rows 16 wide, stinger and blurb lengths).

The perf log: the station build prints `[perf] f= host_ms= half= hud_calls= hud_kb= cmp_kb=` every 5 s (the host frame delta average, the render fallback, the HUD uploads of the window: sub-image calls, KB sent, KB compared) and `[mem] f= match= used= arena= sbrk=` at every countdown. A soak is a rig run of a script that loops the evening (lock with A, A on the vote, hold A in the fight, A on results and the rematch) with the `[mem]` lines compared match to match; `used` must be flat from the second match on. To find a leak, wrap the allocator in a probe build (`EXTRA_CFLAGS="-Wl,--wrap=malloc,--wrap=calloc,--wrap=realloc,--wrap=free" EXTRA_SRCS=probe.c`, the probe records every live block with `G.match_index` and `G.phase` and dumps the blocks of the previous match still alive at the next countdown, grouped by phase and size): the size of a leftover names its struct, and the phase names the code path (an allocation in `game_start_match` is tagged with the vote, the phase before the countdown).

Every build must pass with no new warnings. Look at every screenshot you take. If the console prints a missing render state (`t3_renderer_generated_missing`, logged every 120 frames), append it to `tools/states.txt`, regenerate, commit.

The rig scripts in `tools/scripts/` are shared: `2p-fight.json` (seats 1 and 2, ROLL OUT and CHOP, THE LASER ROOM), `4p-fight.json` (four seats, THE BAY) and `keyboard-solo.json` (pad 0 with d-pad bits and buttons only, as the station keyboard: lobby, a Y tap and two arrow rows in the garage, Start to lock and go, A on the vote, then a d-pad drive with a flipper and a boost). Their frame numbers are the acceptance frames of spec section 18, with two shifts since the controls pass: A locks on the press (no 1 s hold), so the garage shots of the 2p and 4p scripts are at 240 and the vote opens at 249; and nobody has voted when the vote opens, so every script presses A on the vote (308 for the 230 lock scripts) to keep the fight start at 668. The fight segments of the 2p and 4p scripts are generated by `build/balance --make-fight` from the cart's `[sim] setup` line (seed 1401181144 under the rig) so a real AI fight with hits and a KO replays bit exact; regenerate them after any sim or AI change or their hashes drift from the cart. The automatic rules flash on a fresh save plays after FIGHT or SOLO on the title, so every script's A at 60 skips it and the later frames stand; scripts with no input after the fight reach the lobby 40 s after the last input.

## Platform facts

- Pads: every pad is polled every frame; a seat is claimed on an A, B, X, Y or Start edge; `connected` is never read. `read_pads` turns the d-pad bits into full left stick axes (both sides for a TANK seat) whenever a d-pad bit is down, whatever the stick reads, so the station keyboard (pad 0, bits only) drives and a drifting stick never swallows the d-pad; the sim only ever sees axes. `PADS[i]` carries the current and previous `pad_state_t`, the edges, and menu navigation events with repeat (18 frames then every 8). `pad_state_t.buttons` uses the `IN_*` bits, which equal `WC_BTN_*`.
- Resolution: `wc_negotiate_resolution` with a 1280 x 720 cap; `RTR_W`, `RTR_H` are the renderer size; every rect in the game is authored in 1280 x 720 reference space (`rect_t`) and scaled by `rect_scale` at the viewport call. The HUD is authored at 1280 x 720 whatever `HUD_DIV` is.
- The frame: `acc += dt * G.timescale`, up to four `sim_step`s per frame, `alpha = acc / DT` for `world_sync`, the event drain after each step, `render_frame`, `draw_hud`, `hud_upload`, the HUD quad, time based audio frames with a fractional carry.
- The HUD upload (main.c `hud_upload`): the pixel writers in hud.c record what they touch per 8 row band (columns too), `hud_clear` wipes only what the previous frame painted and marks it, and `hud_dirty_bands` hands main.c the bands of the frame. The HUD textures are three GL objects the cart makes itself in wc_init (sRGB format, nearest, clamp; three.c samples them through `t3_texture_from_gl` and never uploads): a ring, so the texture written each frame was last read three frames ago and the driver never waits for the GPU on a partial write (a sub-image write to a busy texture stalls on Mesa V3D, and on the rig's host it cost 0.78 ms a call with one texture against 0.42 with three). Each ring entry keeps a shadow of its content and the bands it still has to catch up on; on a dirty frame the next entry gets its pending bands merged into rects (`hud_band_rects`: runs of bands whose columns nearly agree, so the seam down the middle, the clock chip and the strips are separate rects), each rect's bands compared against the shadow, only the bands that differ sent with `glTexSubImage2D` (texture unit 31, packed through a 192 row staging buffer, `t3_renderer_reset_bindings` after), and the quad's material is pointed at it. The compare is what makes the gate real for the menus, which repaint a full backdrop every frame. `hud_dirty()` is a no-op kept for the drawers; a hint wider than what was written would only widen the upload. Memory: three shadows and the staging buffer, 11.8 MB at HUD_DIV 1, 3 MB at HUD_DIV 2.
- The split-screen recipe is in `render_frame` (world.c): scissor test on, auto clear on, per viewport equal viewport and scissor with y from the top, then scissor off, full viewport, auto clear off for the HUD pass. No shadow maps.
- Lights are fixed at `l1201`: one hemisphere, one sun, two points. An arena with one accent light sets the other's intensity to 0; it is never removed.
- `save_t` is `SAVE` in main.c, magic `RTR1`, crc over everything after the crc; all zero means empty and `save_defaults` resets it.
- The 10.4 strip rects collide: an HP bar of 400 px from x 52 ends at 452 and the F_MONO_L number right of it runs under the weapon gauge at 470. The demo strip uses a 340 px bar with the number at 400; the HUD agent should keep that or move the gauge.
